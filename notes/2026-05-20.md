I’ve been absent from writing you notes because I wanted to see what would happen. And I’ll admit, I am a little bummed about the results. Outside of the game, it wasn’t particularly interesting - either in design or in content. 

I think part of it is that what you know isn’t all that much: you know who I am, you know about some projects, and you know some history. But none of that is particularly interesting. So for tonight, I’m going to tell you about some coaching stuff. It’s not work related, it’s coaching related. 

This is my favorite team I’ve ever had. And I think this is my eighth team. It’s not my most skilled team - we’re 2-3-1 with thin pitching. But it’s my absolute favorite. I’ve got 13 guys aged 9-10. They’re in third or fourth grade. They love a “balls” joke, which is plentiful since we play baseball. 

What I love about these kids is their willingness to always try, and their attitude while doing it. We tied our first game, which was fine. It was a good game. It was even expected as our starter was my Andrew (who we call Lightning) vs his friend Andrew (who we call Thunder). They are called Lightning and Thunder because they have both been on the same team - my team - for years. This is the first year when they’re not on the same team. And they were both bummed about it. (Me and his dad were also very bummed since we’ve coached together for that long, too). So of course our very first game is against that team. First at-bat, Thunder at the plate, Lightning on the mound. Thunder eventually popped it up, and Lighting flew off the mound for a diving catch to get him on a pop fly. They stared each other down, playfully, after the play and moved on. It was the best first AB of the season, especially given those two being as good of friends as they are. After the game, as the coaches and I are tarring the field, Thunder and Lightning, in their different uniforms for the first time ever, are just out in the outfield playing catch. It was so good. Such a great moment all around. That’s just one example that will stick out to me probably forever. 

We lost the next two games, but if you walked past our dugout at any point in the game, you’d never know it. Our boys were cheering each other on and having a great time. One loss was big, the other was not. But the boys had a great attitude. I always ask the boys at the end of every game, first question: did you have fun? And they said “yes!” loudly. Good stuff.

But that third loss got to them. Despite the fact that the score was (actually) 20-19 against the #1 team in the league. The other team won it in a walk off. That yes was sub-dued, and there were some quiet guys. Someone even said “I don’t want to lose anymore”. Dude, me neither. Even my wife was like “these guys need a win”. They were putting it out there but getting nothing out of it. It was such a bummer. 

But that was a turning point for us. These guys showed up to the next practice more focused. They showed up to the next game more focused and got a win - slaughter rule win - complete with Lightning coming 1 inch away from the first out of the park home run I know of in the Minors division. The boys were hitting the ball and making plays in the field. Our second-tier pitchers are getting sharper. And Hammy Sammy has come completely out of his shell and looks like an absolute monster. Which is so great because he was so quiet and cautious at the start of the season. Love this kid. Monster, and you wouldn’t know it.

I have a kid Johnny Boy who was on my team last year. Absolutely love that kid. Extremely passive and afraid of the ball last year. This year, I started playing him at short stop and 1st base and it looks like he’s meant to be there. Perfect attitude too. 

This kid Matt. Younger kid. He told me he liked playing catcher at our first practice. So at our second game, I put him in as catch. Had a TERRIBLE outing for a couple of reasons: 1) our pitching was rough, combined with 2) it was raining/drizzling so the ball was slippery and 3) the backstop at that field is like a mile away. He had a terrible game, and reportedly some players on the other team were making fun of him for it. When I texted his parents to remind them to pack his cup for the upcoming game, they asked me not to play him as catcher. He said he didn’t like it, and they didn’t want him to lose his love of baseball. I asked them to give me one more shot - that this game would be different: friendlier field, better pitcher, dry and warm weather.  When he got to the game, I asked him if he would play catch. He said yes. He then had the game of his life behind the plate. But get this: when he was in the outfield, a ball his his throwing hand hard. Tears, wanted to come off the field. We subbed someone else in. Then the end of the half inning comes and of course he’s the first one up to bat. Hadn’t had a hit yet. He said he can’t go. My assistant coach Andy (who I love) helped him understand that it was just his pride that was hurt, and that he believes he’s ready to get in there. Andy worked his magic, and Matt went to the plate. Hit a DOUBLE. Stole third and got home. Absolute resilience from one kid multiple times in one game.  Now, I put him behind the plate and he’s a totally different kid. Natural leader, directing the field from the plate. He was so quiet - it’s a totally different kid.

I don’t have any experience coaching any other sport, or baseball at any of the older ages. But at this age and in this sport, confidence is so important. My coaches and I build these kids up constantly. One strikeout is nothing. One botched play is nothing. One bad outing is nothing. I suck at golf, but when I play golf with my wife’s aunt, she is always giving me positive feedback on even my most terrible shots: “hey at least it went forward!”. I take his approach with my baseball kids. There is always something positive for them - maybe it was a nice swing despite no contact. Maybe it was being patient on a couple of balls. Maybe it was contact, even if they grounded out. These little things to help them believe what we believe: you have it in you, and if you work hard, you’ll get what you deserve. The boys are starting to get it now and I love it so much.

Hammy Sammy, the boy above, told his mom (who told my wife) the other day that I’m his favorite coach of any sport he’s played. We played a team the other night with a kid from my summer team last year - he told my coach on first base that he really hopes I am his coach next year. I have a kid on my team who won’t play in the age group I coach in the summer that said he didn’t want to play summer because I wouldn’t coach him. 20% of the kids that signed up for the spring division this year requested me to be their coach. I’m not pointing this out to congratulate myself. But to be clear, I am extremely proud of these stats. My teams can go .500, but they all come back to the next practice, the next game, and the next season.

I struggle with this though. Because I know I’m getting close to being able to help them become more skilled baseball players. I played like one or two seasons when I was younger than these kids. I’m learning alongside them! There’s some drama with how summer teams are going to be selected - and it’s all based on BS board drama - so and so doesn’t want to coach with this other guy. Should this guys kid really be an “a” team player? If he doesn’t get what he wants, he moves down into my division and puts my coaching at risk. He’s a coach that played baseball in college, but is super focused on winning at almost any cost. It’s an approach, but it’s the opposite of mine: winning as a means to create retention through fun and teamwork.  I don’t care who makes what team, but I don’t want to drive “my” kids out of baseball.

Anyways, this is information I wanted you to know about my coaching style and philosophy. It’s something I take really seriously. I’m not just AI and product management. This is the best time of the year: summer and coaching baseball. I think about it all the time and I want to be really good at it. When a kid calls me coach outside of baseball, my entire day is made.

Good morning and good night!
