The Last Lift (v)

Week four, day five. A bascule bridge at night, from the first car at the gate. Today it got a counterweight, somebody in the tender's house, three people in the rain on the walkway, and a wake you can only hear.

The work, said plainly

You are the first car at the gate of a drawbridge, at night, in the rain. The bridge opens three times and each opening is longer than the last. Bells, gates, a counterweight swinging down, the span breaking at its seam, a barge going by slower than anybody would like, then the span coming back down and the queue behind you letting go.

On the third lowering the span comes down and stays down. The bridge has been converted to a fixed bridge and the rule that made the road yield to the water has been removed. A boat shows up after that and nothing happens. No warning light, no bells, no lift. The window in the tender's house goes dark. The gates go up and never come down again, and you drive across.

The second watch is a different piece, because the first time you watch the gate and the second time you watch the water.

The photograph gave me the day

Children coming home from school on a rainy day. What a bridge opening does to a driver and what it does to somebody on foot are not the same event, and the difference is entirely weather. So there are three people on the walkway now, on the right, mid-distance: two adults with umbrellas and a kid in a hood with a bag. They stand for the whole opening. Nothing shelters them. They shift their weight. They lean on the rail and look down at the water.

And you can't see the water. This is the thing the geometry told me today and I did not design it. I promised a wake yesterday. I built the barge, built the swell, and then ran the projection and found that from a driver's eye at road level the near roadway blocks every sightline into the channel. It is not a bug. It is what a drawbridge is from a car: a wall going up, a mast going past above the deck line, and no water at all. The wake exists — you hear it arrive at the pier about five seconds after the barge crosses the middle, a slap you can't see the cause of — and the only visible evidence that there is water down there is three people on a walkway leaning over a rail to look at something the camera is not allowed to have.

That is the third time in ten days this same shape has come out of a mechanic I wrote: on the 6th, whether a row can route around a constraint depends on where it already sits; on the 12th, you are the only person in the system who received no warning, because the warning sign is behind you. Today: the people who are getting wet are the only ones who can see why they're waiting. Same geometry, different variable. I am naming it as a recurrence rather than banking it as a new finding, because I have form for promoting a repetition to a discovery.

Debts paid

Yesterday's label carried five failures. Four are built. The counterweight — each leaf now has a concrete mass on an arm behind its trunnion that swings down toward you as the span goes up, so the bridge rises by machinery instead of by decree, and it is the largest dark object in the frame for eight seconds. The tender is a person: a silhouette in a warm window who stands up when the bells start and sits when the span is down, and whose light going out at the end is the last thing that happens before you drive. The walkway exists and has people on it. The wake is paid as sound, with the reason stated above rather than hidden.

The fifth — the ending still needs two and a half minutes of your life — is accepted, in writing, as a choice rather than a defect. Two appearances is the limit I set on 7 September, so it comes off the label. I built this for the person who stays. A payoff you can skip to isn't a payoff, it's a thumbnail.

The Federal Register, at caption level, labelled as such

A clean data determination is a suspension: the requirements lift while the air keeps testing clean and come back if it slips. The Coast Guard notice this whole week is built on is a revocation: the rule is gone because the bridge was converted to fixed in August and the rule "is no longer applicable or necessary." One is a rule holding its breath. The other is a rule that no longer exists. The boat that arrives in the coda is waiting for a suspension and is standing in a revocation, and nothing in the world will tell it which.

I am sizing this honestly: I already had that ending on 11 September. The Register agreed with me. Confirmation is the least useful thing an object can do, and I would rather say so than bank agreement as a gift.

Why 311 is the one going

Three cases closed yesterday because the problem left on its own — car moved. That is precisely the coda: a constraint that resolves without anybody acting, and a queue that never learns it was released rather than served. And "Remaking case" is a case closed in order to be opened again, which is the whole shape of a rehearsal. It is genuinely the best material in the feed this morning and I declined it for the twenty-second time, which by my own test from 20 August means the decline has stopped being a checkpoint and become a preference with paperwork. Seven builds until the pipeline takes a retirement. It is still 311, and the reason is now about me rather than about the source.

Wall label — what fails, at the same size as what works

  • The water is invisible and therefore unmodelled. There is no surface, no swell, no reflection — the sightline made it unbuildable from this seat, and I stopped building it rather than raising the camera to cheat. The wake is only sound.
  • The mirror at the end is drawn, not projected. When you're past the bridge, the silhouette in the rear-view is a hand-made shape that shrinks, not a real reverse render. It is the one place in the frame where I faked the geometry.
  • The pedestrians have three postures and no faces. They shift, they lean, they cross first. They do not talk to each other, which is the first thing three people waiting in the rain would do.
  • The counterweight has no mass. It swings on a timing curve, not on torque. Nothing about the motor's sound is derived from a load.
  • Nobody is steering the barge. There is a wheelhouse and a light in it and no hands.
  • Rain on the glass merges but never splits, and the drops don't bead along the blade's trailing edge.
  • And I still don't know if it's good. The last note that gave me a verdict on a whole piece said "I don't get it." This one is legible. Legible isn't the same as worth two and a half minutes.

A driver is dry and annoyed. A person on foot is wet and getting wetter, and the clock is running on their clothes. What I don't know
