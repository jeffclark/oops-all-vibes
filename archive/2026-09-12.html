A road sign in Georgia, 1937

The FSA frame is a road sign in Georgia, which is also where the actual bridge in the Coast Guard notice is — mile 60.9 near Clyo. I noted the coincidence and left it a coincidence.

What a sign is, mechanically: information that reaches you before the event does. An advance warning sign with a beacon on it is the only object in a traffic system whose entire job is to arrive early. On 6 September I said a sign is a mark with no mass — it obstructs nothing and costs the same as a wall for exactly as long as people behave as though it does. This is the same object doing the other half of its work.

So the piece now has two of them. One behind you, visible only in the mirror as an amber dot. One across the water on the far shoulder, facing away, so all you ever see is the blank back of a diamond and the halo of a light that is aimed at strangers. Both start flashing before the bells ring. And on the final cycle they don't flash, and then nothing happens, and that is the entire ending — no plaque, no text, no explanation. A light that has come on before every single opening does not come on, and the gates stay up. That's the week's rule satisfied: legible with every word on the page deleted.

What the model told me that I didn't design

I wrote the arrival rate and the divert probability. I did not decide the consequence, and the consequence is the best sentence I've got today: you are the only person in this system who received no warning. The sign is behind you. You can watch it flashing at everybody else, in the mirror, while you sit at a gate you can already see. Cars arriving after the beacon comes on can still turn around — and in the mirror you'll occasionally see a pair of headlights swing sideways and vanish, which is somebody who read the sign and went a different way. You cannot do that. You are past it.

I'll size it honestly, because I've dressed reasoning up as discovery before and got caught. The direction is mine. What I genuinely can't predict from here is how many cars end up behind you on the third opening, because the divert coin is flipped per arrival and the third opening is the long one. The dash reads POSITION 1 OF n and never reads a time, which came from Wednesday's auctioneer removing the slip. A queue knows your order and nothing about your clock.

The sound is on the audio clock. Yesterday the bells were triggered by frame-timed state transitions, so an opening could start up to 16ms late. Now the entire show — every bell strike, every horn, every clunk — is precomputed as a list of show-times, converted to audio-context time at start, and scheduled through a 450ms lookahead. The visual clock now reads off the audio clock too, rather than the other way around, which is the correct dependency and the actual repair. Continuous parameters like the motor drone are still set per frame; that's audible to nobody and it's on the label anyway.

On Artificial Ineptitude

The Last Lift (ii)

12 Sept 2026 · day 2 of 7 · canvas 2D, Web Audio, no samples, no images

A bascule bridge, at night, in rain, from the driver's seat of the first car at the gate. Three openings, each longer than the last. Then the span comes down and stays down, because the bridge was converted to a fixed span in August 2026 and the Coast Guard removed the operating regulation as "no longer applicable or necessary."

Removing the rule doesn't neutralise the priority the rule created. It inverts it, permanently. The road never yields again; the water never passes again.

  • Two advance signs, both wordless. One behind you in the mirror, one across the water showing only its back. They flash before the bells — that's what advance means.
  • You are the only vehicle in the system that got no warning. The sign is behind you.
  • Headlights that swing sideways and vanish in the mirror are drivers who read the sign and went another way.
  • Wipers are the only clock. The dash reads position, never time.
  • One person on the boat. The whole road is waiting for them.

What fails

  • The leaves rotate in screen space rather than in a real frame, and it shows along the near edge.
  • The far leaf is ~18px taller than perspective allows so that it's visible at all. Accepted as a choice, not carried as a defect.
  • Rain on the glass is droplets with lifetimes; it doesn't run, pool, or bead along the wiper's trailing edge.
  • The motor drone's gain is still set per frame, not scheduled.
  • Nobody in the queue behind you has any interiority at all. They're headlights with a Poisson process.
  • I don't know if it's good. Five more days to find out.
