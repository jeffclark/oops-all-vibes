What you're looking at

Eight cans on a rail. In the tester's left hand, a printed route card from the creamery: one row per farm, and a count of pips saying how often that farm gets sampled. Most rows carry one pip — dip it every day. One row carries four. That farm has been clean for a long time, so it is tested one day in four, which is how a sampling programme spends a finite tester.

Today is that farm's day. He dips it. The dip is accurate. It reads clean. The can tips into the tank under the floor, and from that moment the fault in it belongs to everybody and to nobody.

The fault has settled to the bottom. A dipper takes from the top. Every word he will write tonight is true.

The only honest signal in the room is the wobble. A can with sediment in it rings slower and wider than a clean one when the rail shakes under a footstep. It is free, it is continuous, it is visible from where you are standing. It is not on the card and there is no column for it.

The claim I'd defend in a bar

The Fish and Wildlife Service decides on the best scientific and commercial data available. That standard is honest and it indexes to availability, which is a property of who went looking and where — not a property of the river. Nothing downstream carries a marker saying which shape that was. So a true entry and a true entry made by a dipper that samples the top are the same ink.

The thing I didn't design

I wrote the sediment decay separately from the wobble. Sediment settles out of suspension over time; the wobble's amplitude comes from the settled mass at the base. Which means the visual tell grows stronger at exactly the rate the instrument goes blind. The clean reading and the obvious rocking are the same quantity seen from two positions, and only one of them has a form to go on.

And the part whose magnitude I can't predict: how fast he reaches the four-pip can depends on how many skips the card hands him earlier. On runs where he's quick, the fault is still suspended and the dip catches it. On runs where he's slow — because he dipped more cans upstream — it has settled and the dip comes back clean. Thoroughness earlier buys a clean reading later. The simulation knows which run you got. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody on that platform could have known either.
