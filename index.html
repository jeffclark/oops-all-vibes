Wall label — findings

The watches share an error. Everybody on that verge set their watch off the same clock in the same village. So each person's error has two parts: a private part that differs, and a common part that doesn't. Averaging two watches removes some of the first and cannot touch the second.

Which means talking raises agreement and leaves accuracy exactly where it was. A group can talk itself into unanimity about a time that is wrong, by precisely the mechanism that was supposed to fix it — and the more of them agree, the more certain each one becomes. The only cure for a shared error is a stranger. The only stranger on that road is driving the bus, and he doesn't stop long enough to be asked.

And the testimony is filtered before it's spoken. Whoever boarded is gone. Whoever has been failed most often is, by Thursday's mechanic, the most likely to have already left. So the people still available to answer how long have you been here? are a selected population, and what they transmit is discouragement. What survives the carry is not what was cut.
