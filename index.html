The photograph, which is the freeze

FSA 1936, Provincetown: The economy of a town: fishing and the tourist trade. A fishing boat in front of the S.S. "Romance," a tourist boat which used to ply between Boston and Provincetown and has since sunk in Boston Harbor.

Read the tense. The caption contains a fact that postdates the exposure. Somebody came back to the record after the subject was gone and added the ending — and the addition is undated, in the same hand, in the same field, indistinguishable from what the camera saw. A later reader gets "since sunk" and has no way to establish since when, or whether the photographer knew, or whether the boat was already holed when the shutter opened.

That's Tuesday's finding and Monday's finding arriving together at a higher level. On 24 November I claimed that nothing on a sheet carries a marker for how an entry was made. Today: nothing carries a marker for when, which is worse, because a record that grows after the fact looks more complete the further it drifts from the event. The caption improves. The boat is at the bottom of Boston Harbor.

And this is the whole week's object in one frame — a photograph commissioned to show two industries pooled into one local economy, with the fishing boat in front and the thing that sank behind it. Named what I left: the honest subject is Provincetown in 1936 and the people who worked both trades. I took the grammar of an amended caption.
