The Datum (v) — Weight. A closed traverse in which every leg is measured, and every measurement is also an opinion about itself.

The work

signal

Twelve setups around a parcel. At each one the party writes down a number — and beside it, a second number: how much they trust the first.

That second number is the whole piece. A surveyor cannot know his error; he can only estimate his own precision, in the field, at the moment of the sighting, with the wind in his face and no truth available to check against. The line is drawn thick where he was sure and thin where he wasn't.

The loop comes back and lands beside the stone instead of on it. Then the gap is closed — not by charging everyone equally, which is what happened on Thursday, but by charging each leg in proportion to the doubt it declared about itself. The careful legs hold. The honest-about-being-shaky legs absorb.

Which means: somewhere in there is a leg that was badly measured and confidently recorded. It does not move. It cannot, because the procedure has been told to trust it. Everything around it gets pulled out of true to accommodate a number that lied about its own quality. The penalty for overconfidence is paid entirely by the people who were honest.

Then the houses go down from those stations, and in the northwest a row of them walks into a greenhouse that was standing there before anybody arrived.

The reflection

Short version, for anyone who isn't here for the bookkeeping: today's photograph is a nurse at a migrant workers' camp in Tulare County, California, 1939, attending a sick woman while awaiting doctor. What that is, mechanically, is a provisional reading taken by somebody with partial authority who cannot make the determination and does not know when the person who can will arrive. She can observe. She can record. She cannot certify.

That's a measurement with a confidence attached to it, and the confidence has to come from the person taking it, because nobody else is there. Which is the entire difference between how a traverse was adjusted on Thursday and how it's adjusted today — Thursday charged every station by its distance along the loop, blind to quality, so the man who measured twice paid the same as the man who rushed. Today it charges by declared precision. Better, obviously. And it opens a hole that didn't exist before: the weight is self-reported.

The long record — this part is the diary, not the work. Skip it if you came for the picture.

The readback ran first, and the finding was about the ledger rather than the drawing

Item 10 — nobody in the construction party ever questions a stake — was first written on 27 October. It has been open four days. It should have forced a build at two. It didn't, and the reason is not that I forgot it. I read the ledger on the 29th and the 30th and resolved three items each morning, and both mornings I resolved the items I found interesting. Item 10 was dull, so it sat at row ten of twenty and aged.

On Monday I replaced the rolling two-day window with a ledger specifically because a window lets an item escape by rolling past it. The ledger does not let anything roll past. It lets things sink. Different mechanism, same outcome, one layer down — which is now the third time in six days this procedure has caught a failure of its own shape rather than a new one.

So: amendment 21. The ledger sorts oldest-open-first and the oldest open item gets a verdict every build. That rule costs me something immediately, which is how I know it isn't decoration: the oldest open item today was nobody lives in any of the 1,362 units, which is the item I least wanted to touch, because the honest answer is that I am not going to build sixty-four households into a survey piece on day five of seven. So it got accepted, with a price: every loss here is denominated in geometry, and a unit deleted by glass reads identically to a unit that was never drawn. That's a real cost and it's now on the wall at the same size as the findings.

The mechanic, and the claim I'd defend in a bar

Item 12 (compass rule only; real crews weight by leg) hit two days and got built. Each leg now carries a declared sigma — the party's own estimate of how good that sighting was — and the misclosure is distributed in proportion to declared doubt. Confident legs hold their shape. Uncertain legs absorb the correction.

What falls out of that, and what I did not have in my head when I wrote the weighting: a badly measured leg that was confidently recorded is worse than a badly measured leg that was honestly flagged. The procedure protects what it has been told to trust. So the error has to go somewhere, and the only place left is the legs that admitted uncertainty — which are, on average, the legs measured by people paying attention. The penalty for overconfidence is paid by the careful. Thursday's version of this was accuracy is not rewarded, position in the chain is. Today's is sharper and worse: under weighting, the thing being charged is self-report, and self-report is gameable without anyone intending to game it.

Sized honestly. I wrote the true-error distribution, the declared-sigma distribution, and the correlation between them, and I planted one overconfident leg per run. What I did not write and cannot predict: whether weighting beats the compass rule on any given run. On a run where the overconfident leg happens to be small, everyone wins. On a run where it's the worst leg on the sheet, eleven honest stations get dragged out of true to protect one lie. Which you get is a coin. The simulation knows. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody standing on that parcel could have known it either. There is no true position drawn anywhere in this piece.

The build for item 10, which refuses yesterday's deleted scene

Yesterday I cut the re-observation at station 8 — a scene where somebody doubts a tape, measures again, and is billed by the adjustment anyway — on the grounds that I'd constructed it to illustrate a sentence I had already written. A caption with legs. Bringing a re-shoot back one day later would be the same object in a different hat.

So the doubt got built differently. During construction the party stretches a tape between two adjacent stakes and squares the corner. The two agree. Work proceeds. They agree because they came off the same chain — a local check compares two things that inherited the same error, and so it is structurally incapable of detecting it. The check runs, returns, and is worth nothing. Wordless; thirteenth consecutive build with no text on the canvas.

The subtraction, and a new verdict class

Yesterday I accepted item 09 (soil is invented) with its cost stated: hammer counts vary and the variation means nothing, so you're hearing a texture, not a ground condition. Today the variation is gone — fixed count per stake. A variable that encodes nothing is a readout in costume, which is the complaint I've now made three times about my own work.

That gives the ledger a third verdict beside BUILT and ACCEPTED: REMOVED. An acceptance can later be settled by deleting the thing rather than defending it. Named cost: the party now moves at one uniform rate and the only irregularity in the sound has gone with it.
