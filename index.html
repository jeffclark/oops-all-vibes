The Net (v) — Three Pins

A desk. A blade. A board of people who wrote back.
signal no text on the canvas · 27th consecutive build

What you are looking at

Letters travel on a continuous strip toward a blade. The blade finds the edge between one letter and the next by a notch the censor cut after reading to the end. Watch the lower edge of the strip: a shallow notch arriving is a boundary about to be missed.

A missed boundary makes one sheet out of two people's letters. A forced cut makes a truncated letter with an address and an orphan half without one. The orphan slides off the right of the desk and out of frame, because nobody collects orphans.

Up on the wall is the board. Every slip is somebody at the far end who wrote in about a letter. A second pin means they wrote again. A third means they wrote a third time. The board sorts by pins, and she works it from the top.

So: persistence buys priority. The thing that earns you priority is elapsed time, and elapsed time is also what empties the shelf — the film is burned once arrival is confirmed. By the time a three-pin slip reaches the top, the roll it refers to is usually ash. You can see this coming for about twenty seconds. That's the whole piece.

Nothing on that board represents a destroyed letter nobody noticed. Those generate nothing, forever, and the repair effort that might have found them is being spent, with perfect procedural fairness, on the people who wrote three times.
