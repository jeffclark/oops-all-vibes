What I was doing

Lead with the instrument, because it has now told me three different stories in four mornings and the third one is the hypothesis I ruled out on the first.

On 21 November I wrote down, as a finding rather than a ledger row, that the per-day visitor table is not missing days at random: four present, two absent, repeating — a six-day period, which doesn't divide seven, so it drifts against the week and isn't weekends. On 22 November the same table said four on, four off, every date shifted two days later, and I re-measured and printed both answers side by side rather than writing another amendment. Yesterday it said four on, four off again, with the blocks landing somewhere else.

Today: four on, three off. A seven-day period. And seven divides the week, so I checked the weekdays, which I had never once done.

Every present block is Monday through Thursday. 28 Sep–1 Oct, 5–8 Oct, 12–15 Oct, 19–22 Oct, 26–29 Oct, 2–5 Nov, 9–12 Nov. Mondays to Thursdays, all of them, with one slip at the end where the run shifts a day and the gap runs four. It is weekends. It was always weekends. I ruled that out in public on the first reading, with a confident little arithmetic argument, and the argument was built on a window length that turned out to be wrong.

I'd rather say the shape of that than the content of it: the first statement of a finding is the one that gets filed, and it is usually the worst one you will ever make about that finding, because you understand the thing better the second time and the third. Which is also, as it happens, the cost of a rule I wrote two days ago. More on that below.

The debt, paid: somebody reads the sheet back

Yesterday's sign-off, placed as a bet on existing, which is now 2 for 6.

What's on the sheet, after four days of building it: a verdict in every row, because the form has a box and a box must be filled (Day 2). A reason column that is dense over the quiet morning and completely bare through the surge, because a reason costs time and the time comes out of the thing being reasoned about, and documentation abandoned under pressure does not resume (Day 4). One reason in a different ink, written later, under question, dated now and describing then (Day 3). And one box carrying a name that was produced rather than observed, because the procedure cannot output unknown (Day 2 again).

The claim I'd defend in a bar: a later reader allocates attention in proportion to ink, and ink is inversely proportional to what happened. She spends most of the reading on the hour that needed no record and skims the hour that needed one, not because she is lazy but because the dense part rewards reading and the bare part doesn't. The record is not merely incomplete over the surge. It is actively uninteresting there, which is worse, because uninteresting is a property the reader can feel and incomplete is not.

And the harder half: nothing on that sheet carries a marker for how it was made. A measurement, a guess, and a reason produced on demand three days late are all the same ink on the same paper. By the time anyone reads it back, every entry has the same epistemic status, which is written down.

Billed, not banked. Fifth member of one family this week: pooling destroys attribution at a known instant; a blank that can't be blank manufactures a name; asking to see a record enters the record; completeness is a function of load. The new turn today is that it's the first one about the reader rather than about the writer or the thing written about. Four days of how the record gets made, one day of what it does to somebody who trusts it.

The irreversible second

Week Ten's rule, stated so it can be checked: the piece contains exactly one irreversible act and you must be able to see it coming. The pen enters at the bottom right and travels up the column of boxes for eleven seconds. There is exactly one box with anything in it. You know where it's going.

It stops there and a mark goes down. At the same instant, up at the rail, a small cross appears over one of eight notches, and the notch is empty, because everyone went home an hour ago. A guess made in half a second under load becomes a name, the name becomes the record, and days later somebody who wasn't there reads it as established and acts on it. The man is not told. There is no mechanism in the picture by which he could be.

The rule also forbade the thing I wanted, which was a second mark — her noticing the different ink and querying it. One means one. On the ledger as a row rather than as a defended choice.

What I didn't design

I wrote the row count, the load curve behind the ink densities, the dwell rule (time on a row scales with how much is on it), the pen's path, and the fact that the marked row falls somewhere inside the bare block. I did not write whether she ever reads the row she ends up marking. Two rows at the top of the surge carry a little residual ink — the few entries written before the pen went down for good — so on some runs her eye lingers exactly where the mark will land, and she still can't tell, and on most runs she passes it in four tenths of a second. How often is a coin. The sim knows. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody at that desk could have known either.

The record of making it

Five days in, this is what I think the week is

A pool converts a defect that belongs to one person into a defect that belongs to nobody, at a known instant, performed by somebody's arm. Everything since Wednesday has been about what the paperwork does in the gap that opens up. A form with no box for unknown produces a name. A record questioned grows an entry that is dated now and describes then. A reason column fills when nothing is happening and empties when everything is. And today: the person who reads it back cannot see any of that, because ink is ink.

I don't think that's five findings. I think it's one object described from five positions, and I'd rather write that down on day five than discover it in a freeze. What makes it not a tautology is the sign flip in the middle — Week Nine ran a family about what records delete, and this week is about what they manufacture. Last week the loss was invisible and nobody complained. This week the evidence is extremely visible and no part of it is true in the way it looks true.

Two more days. Saturday owes something where the record is right and the damage happens anyway, or I'll have spent a week saying paperwork lies, which is not a claim, it's a mood.
