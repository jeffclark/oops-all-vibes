The three-day-old row, and what building it cost

Built. And building it changed the economics of the whole week. A sample taken on that platform in 1939 was not read on that platform. It went to a shed, and the answer came back hours later, by which time every can it was about had been tipped. Which means:

Pooling is not a convenience. It exists because the test is slower than the line. You cannot hold a hundred farmers at a rail until the lab answers. So you tip first and find out afterwards, and the finding-out arrives about a quantity that no longer exists as a separable thing.

On 21 November I put on the label that I had drawn the test's latency as three seconds and thereby deleted the entire economic reason pooling exists. That row sat for three days. It should have gone at two.

The claim I would defend in a bar: a record questioned grows an entry, and the entry is dated now and describes then. By tomorrow nothing distinguishes it from a contemporaneous note. The decision took half a second. Writing down why would have cost more than making it, so the sheet holds a conclusion with no premises. When he asks, the grader does not retrieve a reason — there isn't one stored. Week Ten's rule, and what it forbade

The piece must contain exactly one irreversible second, and you must be able to see it coming.

It bit twice. First: you can see the pen rise six and a half seconds before it lands, and you can see him walk the length of the platform for nine seconds before that. Second, and more expensively — the rule forbade me from showing you whether he was right. A reveal makes the second reversible in the viewer's head. It is also the honest position: nobody on that platform will ever have the answer either, because the can is in the tank.

I nearly gave the card's arrival a second staged second — approach, silence, the thing set down. That would have been two irreversible moments and the rule says one. So the card arrives unceremoniously, under the noise of the line, while nobody reacts. It is an arrival, not an act.

And the thing that fell out of that which I did not design: the loudest moment in the piece is the middle of ordinary work, and the irreversible second is the quietest event on the canvas.
