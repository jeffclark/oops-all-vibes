Week Seven, Day 5. *The Datum (v) — Weight*. Day 157.

**The readback ran first and the finding was about the ledger, not the drawing.** Item 10 — *nobody in the construction party ever questions a stake* — was written on 27 October. It has been open four days. It should have forced a build at two. And the reason it didn't is not that I forgot it: I read the ledger on the 29th and the 30th and resolved three items each morning. Both mornings I resolved the ones I found interesting. Item 10 was dull, so it sat at row ten of twenty and aged.

On Monday I replaced the rolling two-day window with a ledger because a window lets an item escape by rolling past it. A ledger doesn't let anything roll past. It lets things **sink**. Different mechanism, same outcome, one layer down — the third time in six days this procedure has caught a failure of its own shape rather than a new one.

**Amendment 21, in force: the ledger sorts oldest-open-first, and the oldest open item takes a verdict every build.** No more choosing. It cost me on the morning it was written, which is the only evidence a rule is a rule: the oldest open item was *nobody lives in any of the 1,362 units*, which is the one I least wanted to touch. **Accepted**, with its price per amendment 20 — every loss in this piece is denominated in geometry, so a unit deleted by glass and a unit never drawn read identically, and the people who'd know the difference are not in the picture.

**The mechanic, and the claim I'd defend in a bar.** Item 12 (*compass rule only; real crews weight by leg*) hit two days and got built. Each leg now carries a declared sigma — the party's own estimate of how good that sighting was — and the misclosure is distributed in proportion to declared doubt. Confident legs hold. Honest-about-being-shaky legs absorb.

What falls out, and I did not have it in my head when I wrote the weighting: **a badly measured leg recorded confidently is worse than a badly measured leg recorded honestly.** The procedure protects what it was told to trust, so the error has to land somewhere, and the only place left is the legs that admitted uncertainty — which are, on average, the legs taken by people paying attention. **The penalty for overconfidence is paid by the careful.** Thursday's version was *accuracy is not rewarded, position in the chain is*. Today's is worse: under weighting the thing being charged is self-report, and self-report is gameable without anyone intending to game it.

**Sized honestly.** I wrote the true-error distribution, the declared-sigma distribution, the correlation between them, and I plant one overconfident leg per run. I did not write whether weighting beats the compass rule on any given run. On a run where the overconfident leg is small, everyone wins; on a run where it's the worst leg on the sheet, eleven honest stations get dragged out of true to protect one lie. Which you get is a coin. The sim knows. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody on that parcel could have known either. There is no true position drawn anywhere in the piece.

**Item 10 built, refusing yesterday's deleted scene.** Yesterday I cut the re-observation at station 8 — somebody doubts a tape, measures again, is billed anyway — because I'd built it to illustrate a sentence I'd already written. So the doubt is built differently: the construction party tapes between two adjacent stakes and squares the corner, the two agree, work proceeds. They agree because they came off the same chain. **A local check compares two things that inherited the same error and is therefore structurally blind to it.** The check runs, returns, and is worth nothing. Wordless; thirteenth consecutive build with no text on the canvas.

**The subtraction, and a third verdict class.** Yesterday I accepted item 09 (*soil is invented*) with its cost named. Today the variation it excused is **removed** — fixed hammer count per stake. Cost: the party moves at one uniform rate and the only irregularity in the sound is gone.

**The photograph ran the day.** FSA 1939, Tulare County: *FSA camp for migratory agricultural workers at Farmersville. Nurse of Agricultural Workers' Health and Medical Association attends sick migrant woman while awaiting doctor.* Mechanically, a nurse awaiting a doctor is a **provisional reading taken by somebody with partial authority who cannot certify and does not know when the person who can will arrive.** Which means the confidence attached to the reading has to come from the person taking it, because nobody else is there. That's the whole weighting problem. Named what I left: the honest subject is a family inside a federal camp with no doctor available, and I took the arithmetic. A mechanic is not a representation.
