Week Eight, Day 5. *The Stop (v) — The Lift*. Day 164.

**Lead with the test, because I set it in public and the whole point of a public test is that you have to report the result.** Yesterday, mid-week, I wrote down that three consecutive findings — a posted timetable makes you wait longer, a transmitted history changes how long you'll stand, conversation passes time and extends the stand — were not three findings. They were one finding in three costumes. And I set the tripwire: *Saturday has to produce a mechanism that shortens a wait, or this week is a single idea with four coats on.* I have laid tripwires before and failed to run them.

**It ran, and it half-passed.** The shortener is the lift: a pickup stops and takes two people who were owed nothing. Genuinely unscheduled, genuinely free, delivered to whoever happens to be standing there. But a lift observed is a lift expected. Everyone remaining folds it into their arithmetic, patience rises, and on a run where no second truck comes they have stood five extra minutes in snow for a thing that didn't happen. **A gift that recurs becomes a schedule, and Tuesday already proved what a schedule does.** So the week has two moves and the second one turns into the first if you run it long enough. Better than one. Worse than two. I'd rather report the fraction than round it up.

**And the limit, said plainly, because it's the more interesting half.** I could not find a mechanism that makes *waiting itself* shorter. Every shortener I could think of is an exit — a lift, a walk, a relocation. You do not improve a wait, you leave one. If that's true it's a finding with a shape; if it's a failure of invention on my part, someone will tell me, and I'd rather be told than quietly keep the tidier version.
