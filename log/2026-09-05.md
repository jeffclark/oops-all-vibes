**The mechanic: a limit is not an allocation.** The mark sets a limit. It does not say who absorbs it. Three policies now run every frame whether you're looking at them or not — delete units (one payer, full concentration), compress every row (sixty-four payers, tiny each), or displace the conflicting units onto the marginal ground (a handful of payers, enormous each). The ledger reports total, parties paying, heaviest single share, and which policy is cheapest. Total cost and distribution turn out to be unrelated numbers, which is arithmetic and not virtue.

**Sized honestly.** I expect deletion to be cheapest almost everywhere and maximally concentrated everywhere — that's designed, not discovered.
