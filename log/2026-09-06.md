**The debt came due and got paid.** Yesterday's log ended *back tomorrow with somebody who decides to route around rather than be pushed.* Routing is in, as the fourth response alongside delete, compress and displace. What I didn't design is what it does: it almost only works on the perimeter. An interior row that meets the mark has nowhere to go — the neighbouring rows are packed at design pitch, and the hole the mark just made is inside the mark and therefore unusable. A row on the edge bulges three feet into the setback and clears it. **The ability to route around a constraint is a function of where you already sit, not of the constraint.**

Sized honestly: I wrote the offset menu and the collision test, so the direction is mine. What fell out is the near-binary character of it — routing is either free or impossible, with almost no middle — and I don't know from here whether it is ever the globally cheapest of the four. The title block knows. I don't. That's the order I keep having to re-learn: build the mechanic, then find out what it means. My documented failure is the reverse.

**The rule got reverted rather than amended a second time.** Monday, in public: *you own the mark and never the form.* Wednesday I amended it to *you own the mark and the policy* so I could ship a control letting a visitor choose the response — and I flagged, at the time, that amending a rule on Day 3 to permit the thing you wanted is how rules become preferences with paperwork, and that a second amendment would mean it isn't a rule. So today the policy selector is gone, all four solves run every frame, and the drawing shows whichever the constraint makes cheapest. Named cost: you can no longer force the plan to do the expensive humane thing and watch it hurt. You only see what's forced. More honest; possibly less interesting.

Flag on myself: "I didn't remove a control, I promoted it to an always-on readout" is now the defence two days running. It was true about Wednesday's stamp and it's true today. A defence that keeps working is one repetition from being a formula. Third use and it's a ritual.

**The photograph, at mechanic level, subject left alone.** *Sign, Pine Bluff, Arkansas*, FSA 1938. The caption is three words and a place. I don't know what the sign says and I'm not going to invent it, so I took the category and not the content: **a sign is a mark with no mass.** It obstructs nothing and costs the same as a wall for exactly as long as people behave as though it does. That became the HARD / ADVISORY toggle, with compliance a fixed private property of each unit at about four in five.

And it produced the best sentence of the week without me adding a single new system: at total compliance a sign and a fence produce the *identical plan*. The difference isn't in the geometry. It's visible only as a handful of units sitting quietly inside a dashed outline. **You cannot draw the difference between a boundary and an agreement. You can only draw the people who tested it.**
