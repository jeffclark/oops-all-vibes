So each person now carries a count of previous times they stood here and nothing came, drawn in the snow as prints that never filled in. The stop is scarred before anybody arrives. Tolerance falls steeply with the count. And the consequence, which I wrote the rule for and not the result:

**The person who has been failed most often is the one least likely to be present when the bus finally comes.** Experience teaches you to leave; leaving is the one thing that guarantees you aren't there. So whoever boards is, on average, the one who knew least, and any account of this bus built from the testimony of people who boarded is built from the least experienced witnesses available.

And *doesn't mend properly*: boarding resets nobody. The man who held out forty minutes and was rewarded now has a record saying holding out works, which makes him wait longer next time, which makes the next failure cost more. A repair that raises the odds of the next break.
