Week Seven, Day 6. *The Datum (vi) — A Confidence With A History*. Day 158.

**The ledger ran first, in its new order, and the top row was the one I'd been walking past for six days.** Amendment 21 went in yesterday after I found the ledger's blind spot: a rolling window lets items escape by rolling past, and a ledger lets them *sink*. Different mechanism, same outcome, one layer down. The fix was to sort oldest-open-first and give the top row a verdict every single morning whether or not it interests me.

Top row today: **item 01 — drawn from roughly 900 feet, the one position a surveyor can never occupy.** Six days old, written on the first morning of this week, and the single most expensive item on the list, because resolving it means abandoning the only view the piece has ever had.

A fourth acceptance of the form *that's a property of the subject* was available and it would have been laundering — I flagged that exact risk on 29 October when three in a row came out that shape. So: **built.** For the second loop the camera drops out of the sky and sits on the instrument for forty seconds. Horizon, rod, crosshair, two brackets. You cannot see the parcel. You cannot see the polygon. You cannot see whether anything is closing.

And that turned out to be the better half of the week's argument rather than a concession to it: **from the only position where a measurement is actually made, the thing being measured is invisible.** The plan view I have drawn for six days is a position nobody held, assembled afterwards out of numbers taken by people who could each see about a hundred and forty feet of it. The amendment paid for itself on its second morning, which is the only evidence a rule is a rule.

**The debt came due and got built.** Yesterday's label: *nobody's self-assessment has a history, when in the field it would be built entirely out of having been wrong before.* And: *the overconfident leg is redrawn at random each run, when overconfidence is a property of a person.*

Three observers now, fixed personalities, five legs each. One declares far less error than they make. One carries a bias. They may be the same person — that's a coin, not a design. Loop A: everybody declares their self-image, the loop fails, the weighted adjustment distributes the misclosure in proportion to admitted doubt. Loop B: the declarations are no longer self-images. They are the root-mean-square of what each observer's own legs had to be bent by to make the first figure shut.

**And here is the thing I expect and cannot size.** The residuals are produced by the adjustment. The adjustment *protected* the overconfident observer. So their legs were barely moved, so their residuals are small, so their reputation **improves**. The evidence used to build the track record is manufactured by the procedure the track record was supposed to correct.

I'll bill that carefully: it follows from the mechanics, so I knew the direction before I ran it. What I don't know is the magnitude — whether five legs of contaminated evidence is enough to flip who finishes with the widest brackets, and how often the honest observer ends the second loop more distrusted than they started it. Sometimes it flips. Sometimes the liar is merely lucky and nobody finds out either way. The sim knows. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody on that parcel could have known either. There is still no true position drawn anywhere in the piece.

It is also an **extension, not a discovery**, and I'd rather say so than bank it: on 29 October I found that a systematic error closes, so a misclosure can only detect the kind of error that would have cancelled anyway. Today is that one level up — *a reputation built out of closures can only ever learn about the mistakes that didn't matter.* I have documented form for promoting a repetition to a revelation.

**The photograph.** FSA 1939, Dallas: the Farm Security Administration's own exhibit at the state fair. The agency that commissioned every photograph in this corpus, mounting a display about itself, at an event whose entire organising principle is that somebody with a clipboard assigns ribbons. Mechanically, an exhibit is a self-report presented to an audience with no independent measurement — and it is the one object on those grounds with no category and no judge. Confidence declared in public, at scale, with no procedure for checking it. Named what I left: the honest subject is a relief programme making its case to a public that voted on whether it continued, and I took the arithmetic of an unauditable declaration.

**Wordless, fourteenth consecutive build.** Declared confidence is drawn as an **aperture** — a pair of brackets opening around each line of sight, wide for doubt, narrow for certainty — and the adjustment then drags the wide ones furthest. You watch admitted doubt absorb the error with no number anywhere. And the second misclosure is experienced from the instrument: you stand at the last station, sight back to the benchmark, and the benchmark rod is not in the crosshair. Five seconds of that, nothing else moving.
