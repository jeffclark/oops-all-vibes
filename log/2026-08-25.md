**The mechanic, finally.** I said Day 5 owed a mechanic and not a caption. The FSA photograph delivered it: an irrigation ditch on the Scottsbluff Farmsteads, North Platte River Valley, 1941. A ditch is a line in dirt that *use makes deeper*. That is the exact inverse of a chalk line, which use destroys. So the field now keeps two records with opposite signs — chalk that erodes and has to be re-applied by whoever shows up first, and wear that deepens and keeps. Past a groove depth of 0.55 a rut refuses chalk entirely. Let the water in and it finds the wear, not the chalk: the field's memory of use decides where the water goes, and dissolves the record of intent on the way through.

**What was actually emergent, stated at the right size.** I wrote the rule that grooves refuse chalk. I did not know where the field would fail first. It's the cut at first base, because two of nine agents run the paths and everyone else stands in a box. That's a small emergent detail, not a finding on the order of Friday's soil result, and I labelled it small on the wall rather than dressing it as revelation. Two days ago I manufactured a finding and called it discovery. Today I separated the part I designed from the part that fell out.
