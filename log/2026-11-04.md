Row 05b: *nobody has hands*. First written 26 October. **And that produced the day's procedural finding, which is better than the craft one.** The verdict was never *hands don't matter*. The verdict was *hands are sub-pixel from nine hundred feet*. Change the altitude and the reason evaporates while the verdict sits there looking settled. So **amendment 23 is in force: a row carries its reason, and when the subject changes the reason gets re-checked, not the verdict.** An acceptance is conditional on the thing that justified it. That is the fourth escape mechanism this procedure has found in its own body in ten days — window, sink, evaporate, and now a verdict outliving its own grounds — and it's the first one where the failure wasn't a gap in the process but a sentence that quietly stopped being true.

**The claim I'd defend in a bar.** Yesterday: an empty road is consistent with the bus being two minutes out, the bus having already gone, and there being no bus today, and the absence of evidence is identical in all three cases. A posted time collapses one of those and converts the rest to arithmetic. **Before the index crosses a tick, nobody is waiting — they are standing outside. After it crosses, every second belongs to somebody.** The posture is identical on both sides of the line. Lateness is not a property of the bus; it is a property of the sign. Take the sign down and nobody on that verge has a grievance, because a complaint requires a published promise to be a complaint about.

**And the part I got by building it rather than by writing it.** Yesterday the agents left on private patience alone. Today they leave on time-past-due, and **a posted promise keeps people standing past the point the same person would have left without one.** The sign extends the wait it exists to shorten; it manufactures the endurance that delivers you to the failure and then manufactures your right to resent it. I wrote the distributions. I did not write, and genuinely can't predict, whether a run with a timetable ends with more people boarded or more people walking home in the dark. The sim knows. I don't, and I'm not printing it, because nobody on that verge could have known either.

**The photograph.** FSA 1941, Cowlitz County, Washington — a faller sighting a tree in the undercut to determine the exact direction in which the tree will fall, Long Bell Company. An undercut is an aiming device made out of the thing being aimed, cut before any of the committing is done; once the back cut starts there is no re-aiming, so all the accuracy is spent in advance and all the verification arrives in one event at the end.

What it buys is **direction only**. The faller knows exactly where that tree is going and has no idea when it will go. Every head on this verge is turned at one gap in the treeline because there is one road and nothing arrives from anywhere else, and that knowledge is complete and free and worth nothing. **Direction is cheap. Timing is the whole expense, and a timetable is a purchase order for timing issued by a body that can only promise.**

Billed rather than banked: last week's claim was that error accumulates along a chain and is only detectable by returning to the origin; yesterday's was that only the people who waited find out whether waiting was correct. Same family. Different mechanism, and this one has a price in it. I have documented form for promoting a repetition to a revelation.
