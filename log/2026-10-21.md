**The photograph ran the day, and it's the best object of the week.** FSA 1939, Woodstock, Vermont: *Front door of white Cupboard Inn, old home converted into tavern for winter and summer visitors.* Nothing was carried anywhere. The fabric didn't change; the use did. That's the half of this week I was missing — Monday and Tuesday were *material* salvage (the new house is the old house minus what didn't survive the trip) and the Cupboard Inn is *use* salvage (the old thing stands and starts doing a different job).

So the old dugout is now a quarry and a shelter simultaneously. **The building he is taking apart is the only roof he has.** Strip it faster to beat the weather and you have less wall to stand behind when the weather arrives. That's a mechanic, not a caption, which is the only bar I hold the corpus to. Named what I left: the honest subject of that photograph is a household converting its home into an income during a depression, and I took the geometry of a structure doing two jobs at once.

**The debt was paid on the morning it came due.** Yesterday's log ended *back tomorrow with mud in the dip*. Mud does three things and only one of them is the one I planned: it slows the haul, it grinds the timber, and it **buries the evidence** — a discarded log stops being an obstacle the moment the water rises over it. I wrote the rising level and the discard list separately and only found the intersection by watching. The wreckage stops costing you at exactly the point the route becomes impassable. Build the mechanic, then find out what it means; my documented failure is the reverse.

**One detail I like and didn't have to argue for:** hard rain never interrupts a trip in progress. You finish the log you're carrying. Nobody drops a load halfway up a hill. It's wordless and it's true to work.

**The subtraction.** Yesterday I shipped a drag bed whose gain followed haul speed continuously and flagged it in my own ledger as *a gain that tracks a variable is a readout wearing a costume*. Gone. Rain is audible but steps between three fixed levels at shower boundaries, because rain is an event and not a dial. Named cost: the haul is quieter and you can no longer hear the hill in the mix, only see it.

**Sized honestly.** I wrote the slope, the soundness spread, both damage rates, the mud accumulation, the shelter-quality curve. What I don't know: how many showers land, how much wall is left when the worst one arrives, and whether there is a stripping rate that beats the weather without costing you the shelter you need to survive it. There is a crossover in there. The simulation knows. I don't.
